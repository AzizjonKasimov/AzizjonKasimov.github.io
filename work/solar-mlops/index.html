Solar forecasting and anomaly detection, in production

At Recs Innovation I was promoted twice, from intern to AI Team Lead. I led the team that took solar power forecasting and anomaly detection for solar power plants into production, with the inference services connected to a Virtual Power Plant (VPP) platform.

My role
Data Science Intern → AI Developer → AI Team Lead (Mar–Nov 2024). Led the ML, backend, and frontend teams.
Result
Forecast error from 9% to 7%. Anomaly precision up ~35%, recall up ~37%. Deploys from 3 hours to 15 minutes.
Stack
PyTorch, graph neural network (GNNAD), FastAPI, Docker Compose, GitHub Actions, PostgreSQL, Prometheus, Grafana, MLflow
Business impact
Revenue from power brokerage on the Korea Power Exchange (KPX). 80% less manual ops work (~$48K/year).

The problem

The company needed two models working in production, not just in experiments:

  • a solar generation forecast accurate enough for power brokerage on the Korea Power Exchange (KPX), and
  • anomaly detection that watches data from solar power plants.

Both had to run reliably and plug into a commercial VPP platform.

System overview

Forecasting

  • As AI Developer, I built a PyTorch solar generation forecast with under 10% average error. That qualified the company for KPX business.
  • As team lead, I cut the forecast error from 9% to 7% (22% lower), which enabled revenue from KPX power brokerage.

Anomaly detection for solar power plants

I worked on multivariate time-series anomaly detection with a graph-based model (GNNAD). The gains came step by step:

  • Clearer view of errors. I refactored the open-source codebase heavily and added plots that compare predictions with actual data for every feature.
  • Better error calculation: precision +5%, recall +7%.
  • Hourly thresholds: 24 thresholds instead of one global threshold, to follow how the error changes during the day. Recall +10%.
  • Cleaner training data: removing anomalous points from the training set gave precision +30% and recall +20%.
  • Honest validation: cross-validation that matches production, with 16-point inference windows, hourly thresholds, and nightly retraining.

Overall, precision rose by about 35% and recall by about 37%. Earlier, as an intern, I had tuned the GNN model to 70% precision and 50% recall, tracked the experiments in MLflow, and built the FastAPI endpoints that connect the anomaly detection system to the VPP platform.

MLOps and deployment

  • I containerized the anomaly detection system and automated deployments with GitHub Actions. Deployment time fell from 3 hours to 15 minutes.
  • I built end-to-end MLOps infrastructure with Docker Compose, GitHub Actions, PostgreSQL, Prometheus, and Grafana. Manual operations fell by 80%, saving about $48K a year.

Leading the team

As AI Team Lead, I managed the ML, backend, and frontend teams and made sure the models plugged cleanly into the VPP platform.
