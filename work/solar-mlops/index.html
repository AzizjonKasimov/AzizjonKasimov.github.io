The problem

The company was entering power brokerage, which means selling the output of small solar plants on the Korea Power Exchange (KPX). To take part, a company must forecast the plants' output accurately enough.

The company also needed to notice problems at a plant early, from the readings the plant sends.

So two models had to work in production, not just in experiments:

  • a forecast of each plant's output, accurate enough for KPX, and
  • a detector that flags unusual readings in a plant's data.

Both had to run reliably and plug into a commercial VPP platform.

Anomaly detection for solar power plants

The detector is a graph neural network (GNNAD). It learns how a plant's readings normally move together and flags readings that break the pattern.

As an intern, I tuned its first version, tracked the experiments in MLflow, and built the FastAPI endpoints that connect it to the VPP platform. As team lead, I raised its accuracy step by step and measured each step:

  • A clearer view of errors. I refactored the open-source code heavily and added plots that compare the prediction with the actual reading for every feature, so we could see where the model went wrong.
  • A better error calculation raised precision by 5 points and recall by 7.
  • Hourly thresholds. One threshold for the whole day missed anomalies at some hours, because the error changes during the day. Using 24 thresholds, one per hour, raised recall by 10 points.
  • Cleaner training data. Removing anomalous points from the training set raised precision by 30 points and recall by 20.
  • Honest validation. The cross-validation runs the way production does, with 16-point inference windows, hourly thresholds, and nightly retraining, so a good test score means a good production score.

In total, precision rose by about 35 percentage points and recall by about 37. In plain terms, the detector raised far fewer false alarms and missed far fewer real problems.

MLOps and deployment

  • As AI Developer, I put the detector in containers and automated its deployment with GitHub Actions. A deploy took 15 minutes instead of 3 hours.
  • As team lead, I built the rest of the MLOps setup: Docker Compose services with PostgreSQL, deployments from GitHub Actions, and Prometheus metrics on Grafana dashboards. Manual operations work fell by 80%, which saved about $48K a year.
