AI expense tracker with bank-statement import

Every few days, I spent 30 to 60 minutes tracking my spending by hand. I built an app that reads my bank's statement, lets AI pick a category for each payment, and asks me to check each row. It also tells my girlfriend what she owes me.

The problem

My bank, Toss Bank, gives me statements as password-protected Excel files. Each row has a date, an amount, and a short shop name.

I wanted to know where my money goes, and my girlfriend and I share many costs. So every few days I copied the rows into a budget app, chose a category for each one, and worked out her share.

How it grew

I started small. In April 2026 I built a Telegram bot in two days. It became a Telegram Mini App, a small web app inside Telegram, with an AI import. In June 2026 it became the Android app I use today.

Design decisions

  • Code handles the numbers. Once, the AI read my account balance as income. Now code reads every amount and date, and the sign of the amount decides whether a row is income or an expense. The AI only picks the category, so it cannot change a total.
  • Better input beats a better prompt. Coupang charges all look almost the same on the statement, so no prompt could label them well. Matching each charge to its order email gave the AI the real product names. A receipt counts only when its total equals the charge and the email arrived within two hours of it.
  • My rules come first. Rules I set for shops I know always win over the AI.
  • Failures are never silent. If the AI call fails, or its labels do not fit the list, local rules label the rows and an alert says why. A row that nothing can label stays "Unknown" for me to fix.
  • Decisions come from real data. Refunds used to count as income. In my own statements, 10 of 17 Coupang refunds matched exactly one earlier charge. So the app now takes those off the purchase by itself and leaves the rest for me to match.
  • Private by default. The data lives on the phone. At import, only the statement text goes to the AI, through my own gateway. Backups go to a private GitHub repository and never include passwords or tokens.
